Seedance 2.5 Prompt
Create a 30-second, 16:9 photorealistic live-action science-fiction arena battle. Preserve the bright daytime colosseum look: sunlit sand, strong rim light through dust, monumental stone-and-metal architecture and packed spectator tiers. Keep faces readable in the backlight. Use convincing weight, hard impacts and weathered materials. One location, continuous time.
CHARACTERS AND REFERENCES
@Image 1 = the Executioner, approximately 3.2 m tall. Preserve his pale bald head with a red central stripe, red eyes, black respirator, ivory-and-black armor, red accents, spiked shoulders, back hoses, boots and tabard. His respirator stays on. He has ONE battle-axe with exactly the reference design.
@Image 2 = the Cyborg Beast, approximately 3.2 m high and 4.8 m long. Preserve its four-legged bull body, cloven hooves, dark armor, elongated skull-like metal faceplate, two red eyes, spikes, chains and ornaments. The forehead emblem is not an eye. This image shows its UNDAMAGED starting appearance; later damage permanently overrides its original two-horn silhouette.
The Emperor = one recurring adult man with an uncovered face, a small gold crown and a dark red robe with gold trim, seated on a gilded throne in the elevated imperial box. Establish his face clearly before the fight, then show the SAME man in the ending.
Use the reference sheets for appearance only. Do not display sheet layouts, labels, white backgrounds or duplicate characters.
IRREVERSIBLE HORN STATE
Before the breaking impact: two intact horns attached to the Beast.
At the impact during 19-20s: break ONLY the Beast's anatomical RIGHT horn at its root. In a straight frontal view, this is the horn on the viewer's left.
After that exact impact, through the end: ONE intact LEFT horn remains attached; the RIGHT side has ONLY a short, blunt, jagged stump with a dark mechanical fracture surface. Make the missing-horn gap unmistakable. Keep this damaged side visible in the later combat shots. Camera cuts, changes of angle and the original reference MUST NOT restore or mirror the missing horn.
There is exactly ONE detached right horn. It has the same curve, length, ivory ridges and black pointed tip it had while attached. It has ONE pointed black tip and ONE thick, blunt, irregular broken base. It is not a double-ended crescent, a pair of connected horns or a symmetrical U-shaped object.
The SAME detached horn falls, rests on the sand, is picked up, is thrown and remains embedded in the throne. Its local shape, color, surface markings and broken base never change. Only its position and orientation change.
The axe is a separate object. After the finishing slash, the Executioner plants it in the sand and releases it BEFORE picking up the horn. The axe stays in the arena. The thrown object has no shaft, grip, axe blade or newly added parts.
ACTION, REACTIONS AND CAMERA
The one-second intervals describe choreography, not mandatory cuts. Keep camera movement active in every interval while maintaining readable geography. Show actual contact, not just its aftermath. Use normal speed except for the single finishing slash at 22-23s.
Use a continuous shot across 18-21s to connect the attached horn, the impact, the damaged head and the falling fragment.
Use another continuous shot across 26-29s to connect the pickup, the throw, the horn's flight and its impact beside the Emperor. Then make ONE deliberate cut to his reaction at 29s.
00-01s | Behind the barred gate, exactly two red eyes glow in darkness. A harsh snort blows sand beneath the bars. Camera skims the sand and pushes toward the eyes.
01-02s | Track from the central iron stake along the taut tether to the Executioner's ankle shackle, then tilt toward the axe in his hands and his masked face.
02-03s | AUDIENCE REACTION: a moving medium close-up of three clearly visible front-row spectators. Angry faces, open mouths and forceful thumbs-down gestures; the packed tiers behind them echo the gesture.
03-04s | EMPEROR INTRODUCTION: push toward the seated Emperor's clearly lit, uncovered face. He gives a cold, contemptuous smile and slowly turns his thumb down. Show the gilded backrest beside his head.
04-05s | The Beast smashes through the gate. Retreating low camera catches the breaking bars and its full body charging into sunlight.
05-06s | Track beside its pounding hooves and rattling chains as it accelerates toward the tethered Executioner. Keep the approaching threat and the central stake spatially connected.
06-07s | He cuts the exposed tether chain with one visible downward axe strike. Follow the blade to the broken links as the tension releases.
07-08s | He jumps sideways with the axe. The Beast narrowly misses him and pulverizes the stake. Whip-pan with his escape while retaining the impact in the frame.
08-09s | The Beast brakes and pivots for its second charge. A rising rear-quarter camera move reveals its new attack line and the Executioner waiting ahead.
09-10s | The Executioner drives the axe blade into the sand beside the attack lane, leaving the handle upright. He visibly releases it and raises both empty hands. Push in from his side.
10-11s | He catches one intact horn in each hand. A low three-quarter two-shot shows the contact, bent elbows and the Beast's momentum driving him backward.
11-12s | Track sideways as his boots carve two deep trenches through the sand. The Beast keeps pushing; chains and armor shake under the load.
12-13s | He spreads his stance, drives his heels down and stops the slide. Camera drops to the braking boots, then rises as he twists the horned head sideways.
13-14s | He pivots through his hips and pulls the off-balance Beast onto its flank. Widen with the heavy sideways impact, whipping chains and outward burst of sand.
14-15s | AUDIENCE REACTION: track laterally past the SAME front-row spectators as they spring to their feet, eyes wide, shouting in disbelief. Their contempt has changed to astonishment; the rows behind them rise.
15-16s | Return to the arena. The Executioner strides to the planted axe and visibly retrieves it. Track the handle upward; the Beast scrambles upright behind him in the same frame.
16-17s | The Beast rushes in and hooks its still-intact horns beneath his torso armor, launching him without piercing him. Show the lifting contact before tilting upward with his body.
17-18s | A rising three-quarter camera arc follows him to the apex. He holds the axe in both hands overhead; the Beast remains clearly below him.
18-19s | Begin the uninterrupted horn-breaking shot from the Beast's front-right quarter. The Executioner descends, aiming the axe at the ROOT of its RIGHT horn. Keep that attached horn and the blade visible together.
19-20s | The blade visibly strikes the right horn's root. ONE horn separates with a metallic crack and sparks. Continue the same shot: show the short jagged right stump, the intact left horn and the detached curved horn together. No second horn breaks.
20-21s | Follow the SAME horn as it lands beside the Executioner's landing position, then widen to include the Beast's permanently one-horned head. The horn on the sand has one black tip and one blunt broken base. The Executioner lands holding his axe; the Beast turns.
21-22s | The ONE-HORNED Beast makes its final charge. Track from its front-right side, keeping the short broken RIGHT stump obvious and the single intact LEFT horn visible. The Executioner stays planted.
22-23s | ONE brief slow-motion beat: he turns his torso as the Beast passes and delivers one lateral axe slash through the exposed mechanical drive linkage on its right neck. Track alongside the contact. Its right horn is STILL missing; only metal sparks appear.
23-24s | Return immediately to full speed. The Beast's legs buckle; it slides and collapses into the sand with one intact left horn and the same right stump. Follow the fall as its red eyes fade out.
24-25s | AUDIENCE REACTION: push toward the SAME spectators. Their shouting stops, raised hands lower and their mouths remain slightly open in stunned silence. The whole colosseum becomes quiet.
25-26s | The Executioner plants the SAME axe blade into the sand to his LEFT and releases the handle completely. A moving medium-wide shot shows both empty hands, the stationary axe on his left and the detached horn on the sand to his right.
26-27s | Begin ONE continuous shot. He picks up ONLY the detached RIGHT horn with his RIGHT hand, gripping its thick broken base. Track his hand upward. Clearly show its ivory curve, single black tip and broken base; the axe remains planted separately behind him.
27-28s | Without a cut, he fixes his gaze on the VISIBLE, seated Emperor and throws the horn toward that occupied throne. His left hand is empty. Accelerate from beside his throwing shoulder to follow the SAME curved horn; keep its profile readable.
28-29s | Without a cut or loss of the projectile, track the SAME ivory-and-black horn into the imperial box. The seated Emperor's face and throne are clearly visible. The horn's black TIP embeds in the throne BACKREST immediately beside his face, not in a distant column. Most of the curved ivory horn and its blunt broken base remain outside the backrest. The Emperor recoils, uninjured.
29-30s | CUT TO EMPEROR REACTION: a tight three-quarter close-up shows the SAME Emperor's smile vanish, eyes widen and fingers clamp around the armrests. He freezes in terror while the camera makes a short push-in. Keep the SAME curved horn vibrating beside his face in the frame. End on this unmistakable reaction, not on an empty throne or a pillar.
SOUND
No BGM or musical score. Diegetic effects only: crowd jeers at the start, a shocked roar when the Beast is thrown, then stunned silence after it collapses. Include snorts, masked breathing, heavy chains, pounding hooves, scraping boots, ringing axe metal, breaking iron, stressed machinery and sand impacts. In the quiet ending, emphasize the horn's flight, its hard impact in the throne, the short vibration and the Emperor's sharp involuntary intake of breath. No spoken dialogue, narration or explanatory voices.

FINAL CONTINUITY RULES
Keep the severed ankle tether severed. The axe is planted and retrieved before the aerial attack, then planted again after victory; it remains in the arena during the horn throw and the Emperor reaction. Never put the axe into the throwing hand during the ending.
After the right horn breaks, never show two intact horns on the Beast again. Never switch the damaged side, regrow the horn, attach the ground fragment back to the head or duplicate the fragment.
The detached horn must remain a single curved horn with one black tip and one blunt fractured base in the hand, in flight, embedded in the throne and beside the Emperor's face. No transformation into an axe, spear, sword, handle or two-pointed crescent.
Show the Emperor as an identifiable person before and after the throw. Keep the final impact in the occupied throne backrest beside his face; he is frightened, not physically injured.
No blood, gore, exposed organs, dismembered bodies, magical repair, transformations, additional combatants, energy beams, anime rendering, cartoon styling, plastic-looking CGI, subtitles, captions, watermarks or added logos. Preserve the reference armor emblems. Do not hide the horn fracture or throne impact with dust, shake, a cutaway or excessive motion blur.

Seedance 2.5 Prompt
Complete revised sequence: 30 seconds, 16:9, photoreal live-action epic fantasy cinema.
Restore monumental scale, violent impacts, the furious eye close-up, the rotating thrown sword and the backlit victory.

SETTING AND REFERENCES
Daylight in an enormous ancient Roman-inspired fortress. Towering stone curtain walls with a jagged silhouette of deep crenellations enclose a vast sandy courtyard. The high parapets stand far above the Minotaur. Maintain this immense scale throughout, including the hero's leap from the actual TOP of the high wall. Heavy timber gates, weathered blocks, harsh sunlight and airborne grit. Keep the sun fixed in the world; position the final camera so the sun is behind the hero.

@Image 1 = the ONLY hero, approximately 190 cm tall: short dark hair, beard, cheek scars, muscular body, golden armor, ragged pale cloak, boots and the exact reference sword. He remains bareheaded.
@Image 2 = allied bronze infantry: turquoise patina, crested helmets, round shields and worn cloaks. The defenders also carry ordinary handheld spears. All remain helmeted.
@Image 3 = the ONE approximately 4.2 m Minotaur War King: original bull head, head armor, broad forward-curving horns, bone armor, black-and-white body markings, massive axe, furred shins and cloven hooves. The red circular target stays on his anatomical LEFT upper shoulder.
@Image 4 = enemy infantry: dark iron helmets, chainmail, fur cloaks, dark armor, curved swords and round wooden shields. All remain helmeted.
Preserve identities, proportions, materials and equipment. Reference sheets supply appearance only; their text, borders and layout never appear.

WEAPON CONTINUITY — ONE PHYSICAL SWORD
Before the throw, the hero holds his single sword in his RIGHT hand, including through the wall impact.
During 21–22 seconds, show an actual release: his fingers open, the sword leaves, and his hand becomes EMPTY.
During 22–23 seconds, there is exactly one airborne sword, making a controlled end-over-end rotation in slow motion.
During 23–24 seconds, its POINT enters the center of the red shoulder target first. Most of the blade sinks deeply into the shoulder; the guard, hilt and a short section of blade remain outside. The point is buried, never pointing outward from the body.
From impact until the visible extraction during 27–28 seconds, the sword stays rigidly embedded in the SAME shoulder at the SAME depth and angle relative to that shoulder. It moves with the shoulder as the Minotaur staggers and falls backward. The hero remains empty-handed.
The embedded hilt stays clearly visible in the wider views, during the fall, on the supine body and while the hero climbs onto him. Dust, framing and the hero's body must not conceal it.
During 27–28 seconds, show the hero's right hand grasp that exact hilt and pull that exact blade completely free along its entry axis in one continuous action. Only then does he have a sword again.
The extracted sword is the same object raised in the final pose. Never show both an embedded sword and a second sword in his hand.

OTHER PHYSICAL CONTINUITY
Opening spear attacks strike hard chest armor and the outer shoulder rings, never the red center. Shafts snap outside the body and debris falls away. No arrows, bows or lingering spear fragments in the target.
Each furred shin ends above a solid dark hoof with exactly two distinct halves and a clear central cleft. Keep the hard hoof walls and edges visible below the fur.
Preserve a connected head, neck, torso, pelvis and four limbs. Both large horns remain rooted in the skull with the original shape and forward curvature; they rotate only with the head.
The Minotaur falls BACKWARD and finishes SUPINE, chest and muzzle facing upward. Show that transition, rather than cutting from one body orientation to another. His axe lands flat on his right side, clear of his head and the hero's path.

CAMERA AND RHYTHM
Action-movie camerawork continues every second throughout the entire video.
One-second beats describe evolving action, not mandatory hard cuts. Use forceful tracking, low angles, whip-pans, plunging camera movement and brief impact shake, while keeping contact readable.
The kick, flight and wall collision form a continuous side-tracking action.
Use slow motion specifically for the spinning sword; return to real time at penetration.
From the shoulder impact through extraction, use a connected three-quarter camera move from the marked LEFT-shoulder side; widen for the fall and keep the embedded sword visible.
Finish from a very low angle, then slowly crane upward while arcing around the hero, with the sun behind him. Do not replace this with a static wide shot.

SECOND-BY-SECOND SEQUENCE

00–01s:
Inside the monumental fortress, bronze defenders brace overlapping shields against the buckling timber gate. Push low along skidding boots and shaking shields; towering crenellated walls rise behind them.

01–02s:
The gate explodes inward under the Minotaur's impact. Huge splinters, beams and shields fly toward the retreating camera. Soldiers are knocked backward through dust. Physical destruction, not a fireball.

02–03s:
The Minotaur storms through the breach with his axe raised. Track from a crushing hoof strike up to his horned bull head. Human soldiers establish his size; the much higher walls establish the fortress's scale.

03–04s:
Allies thrust their spears against his chest armor and outer shoulder rings. Shafts buckle and snap as the soldiers recoil. Track laterally through the failed impacts; no weapon stays embedded.

04–05s:
Push into the original red shoulder circle, pristine at its center. Broken spear pieces fall past it without entering it. Pull with the shoulder as the Minotaur winds up his axe.

05–06s:
One enormous axe sweep smashes the overlapping shield line. Shields buckle and several allies are hurled sideways together. Follow the axe through visible contact, then whip-pan with the flying soldiers and debris.

06–07s:
Two sharp reaction inserts: a helmeted ally recoils and runs in terror; another crashes into the sand beneath his shield and struggles to rise. Preserve the frantic momentum.

07–08s:
Dark enemy infantry flood through the ruined gate behind the Minotaur. Sweep across the advancing ranks, then whip-tilt up the full towering wall to reveal the golden hero on its highest crenellated parapet.

08–09s:
The hero launches from the TOP of the high wall into the vast drop. A plunging camera follows beside him, showing many courses of stone racing upward behind his falling body. His ragged cloak lashes violently.

09–10s:
Continue the rapid descent into a thunderous courtyard landing. Sand and loose grit erupt around his boots. Swing low toward his bare face and golden armor as he rises immediately into an attack.

10–11s:
He charges alone into the enemy wave, knocks aside the first shield and slashes across the armored attacker, dropping him. Track at waist height through the contact and follow-through.

11–12s:
He parries a second enemy's curved sword and drives a returning slash across armor. The attacker tumbles backward as the camera moves tightly beside the hero's forward advance.

12–13s:
A third enemy attacks from the flank. The hero pivots beneath the blade and slashes him into the next rank. A fast controlled arc reveals the surrounding attackers without losing the hero.

13–14s:
The hero checks a rear attacker with his left elbow and turns into a decisive right-hand sword strike. Whip-pan with the turn to reveal a cleared lane and the approaching Minotaur.

14–15s:
The Minotaur recognizes him and roars, raising the massive axe overhead. Push low past the hero's shoulder toward the towering beast, horns and axe silhouetted against the crenellated walls.

15–16s:
The axe crashes into the paving as the hero rolls aside. Stone splits and chunks blast across the courtyard. Track the roll at ground level, keeping the blade's impact visible.

16–17s:
The Minotaur drives a devastating kick into the hero's breastplate, launching him violently backward into the stone wall within this beat. Track continuously from contact to flight to back-first collision. Spiderweb cracks burst outward from the impact point; stone fragments and dense sand spray outward.

17–18s:
The hero immediately drops from the wall and collapses onto one knee amid falling debris. His chin stays tucked, back rounded and arms bent; no hovering or seated flight. He still grips the sword in his right hand and wipes blood from his mouth with his left as he forces himself up. The spreading wall cracks remain behind him.

18–19s:
EXTREME CLOSE-UP of his eyes and blood-streaked brow. A thin trail of blood runs along his temple and cheek. He stares directly into the camera with furious, narrowed eyes. Push closer with a tiny controlled camera move; hold the rage clearly instead of cutting away immediately.

19–20s:
His gaze shifts from the lens toward the Minotaur's red shoulder target. Follow the eyeline to its central disk as the giant begins charging. The hero sets his stance and draws his sword arm back.

20–21s:
A low three-quarter tracking shot follows the charging legs. Fur ends above clearly defined two-part hooves; each landing drives a sharp burst of dust outward. Lift the framing to establish the clear line between the hero and the left-shoulder target.

21–22s:
The hero forcefully throws his ONE sword overarm from his right hand toward the charging Minotaur. Show the release and the now-empty hand in the same shot. Follow the sword leaving him, with its target ahead.

22–23s:
SLOW-MOTION CLOSE-UP tracking beside the spinning sword. It makes one controlled end-over-end rotation through sunlit dust, revealing the same blade, guard and hilt from the reference. The Minotaur and red circle remain readable ahead. Its point leads as it reaches the target.

23–24s:
Return to real time as the point drives DEEPLY into the exact center of the red shoulder target. The blade stops rotating and locks into the shoulder; the hilt protrudes outward. In the same moving shot, widen to show the Minotaur's violent recoil and sudden loss of strength. Keep the embedded sword unmistakably visible.

24–25s:
The Minotaur staggers backward, knees buckling under his weight, and drops his axe flat on his right side. Continue the three-quarter move without hiding the left shoulder. The embedded hilt moves with his shoulder at an unchanged angle and depth; the hero's hands remain empty.

25–26s:
He falls BACKWARD: hips descend, his broad back and shoulders slam into the courtyard, and his legs settle forward. A heavy ground impact throws dust outward. His chest and muzzle face upward; his head, neck and horns stay attached and anatomically coherent. The sword still protrudes visibly from the same left shoulder, with its hilt clear of the ground and facing the camera side.

26–27s:
The dust stays below the shoulder line. Without cutting away from the embedded sword, the empty-handed hero steps onto the supine Minotaur's broad torso, establishes his footing and leans toward the exposed hilt. The giant remains motionless in the same orientation. Battle noise briefly subsides.

27–28s:
In a clearly readable continuous view, the hero grips the protruding hilt with his RIGHT hand and pulls the original sword fully out of the shoulder along its entry axis. Show the blade emerging and clearing the body. He straightens on the Minotaur's torso with that sword in hand.

28–29s:
Move into a powerful ground-level LOW ANGLE. Standing on the supine Minotaur, the blood-streaked hero raises the recovered sword toward the sky. The sun sits directly behind him, outlining his hair, gold armor, torn cloak and sword with brilliant rim light. Retain enough reflected fill to read his face and armor.

29–30s:
The camera slowly CRANES UPWARD AND ARCS AROUND him, preserving the backlit heroic pose and the fallen Minotaur beneath his boots. The ally who fled begins striking his shield; surviving bronze soldiers join in thunderous shield blows and wordless cheers. The recovered sword remains clearly raised through the final frame.

SOUND
No BGM or musical score. Only synchronized environmental and action sounds: straining and splintering timber, snapping spear shafts, crushing shields, falling armor, footsteps, heavy hoof impacts, sword clashes, the Minotaur's roar, cracking paving, the explosive wall collision, raining stone fragments, breath, wind and sand.
The flying sword has a moving blade whistle; penetration has a single synchronized impact without musical enhancement. The backward fall produces a massive grounded thud and settling debris.
Briefly reduce battle noise during 26–27 seconds, retaining breath and falling grit. Finish with the sword extraction, armor movement, shield blows and wordless human cheers.
No dialogue, narration, lyrics, subtitles, captions, title cards, logos or UI.

FINAL CONTINUITY CONSTRAINTS
No low-wall substitute for the high parapet leap. No gentle wall bump: show radiating spiderweb cracks, flying fragments and dust.
No seated flying pose, wall hovering or raised spread arms.
No arrows, reversed embedded blades, disappearing sword, duplicate sword or sword returning to the hero before extraction.
No knee-slash replacement for the thrown-sword finish.
No prone final body: the Minotaur ends on his BACK, face and chest upward.
No detached horns, changing horn shape, disconnected neck, fused limbs, fur-ball hooves or isolated ground spikes.
No dismemberment, exposed tissue or blood spray. Preserve the hero's visible blood streaks and furious eye close-up.
No cartoon styling, modern objects, reference-sheet layouts or cuts that skip the impact, backward fall, extraction or final rising camera arc.
